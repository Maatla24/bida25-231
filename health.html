Nationwide Polio Campaign Targets 300,000 Children

Published: April 18, 2026 | Reporter: Leatile Mogende

GABORONE — The Ministry of Local Government and Traditional Affairs, in collaboration with the Ministry of Health, has launched a massive nationwide polio vaccination campaign. The initiative is a proactive response to vaccine-derived poliovirus type 2 (nVOP2) cases detected in environmental samples across neighboring countries.

  • The Rollout: Round 1 (May 5-8), Round 2 (June 8-11).
  • Strategy: Mixed-model approach including schools, clinics, and door-to-door visits.
  • Security: Verify official Ministry name tags on all health workers.

Drug Availability Stabilizes at 64% After Military Intervention

Published: April 22, 2026 | Reporter: Lucia Molete

GABORONE — The national medical supply chain has reached a fragile equilibrium, with essential drug availability stabilizing at 64% following a direct military intervention to secure pharmaceutical logistics. The 64% stabilization mark is a sharp rebound from last month's catastrophic low of 41%, when supply lines fractured under intense bureaucratic gridlock and localized conflicts. Under "Operation Vital Supply," the military took control of major distribution hubs and deployed armored convoys to transport medicines through high-risk corridors. "By securing the highways and warehouse facilities, we have established a baseline," said Defense Ministry Spokesperson Col. Arthur Vance. "Maintaining a steady 64% availability proves that our presence has restored structural order." Despite the progress, a 36% deficit still leaves millions of citizens vulnerable to critical shortages of life-saving medications like insulin and antibiotics. Independent humanitarian groups warn against celebrating prematurely. "We are no longer in total freefall, but stabilization is not a cure," said Dr. Elena Rostova, Director of the Coalition for Public Health. "The military has patched the holes in the delivery trucks, but they cannot fix the underlying issues of manufacturing backlogs and a lack of long-term funding." With data showing a plateau at 64% over the last two weeks, experts believe the intervention has reached the limit of its effectiveness. Government officials have signaled that talks are underway to transition logistics back to a reorganized civilian health ministry by the end of the quarter.

  • Supplies: 12-month ARV buffer secured (73% availability).
  • Aid: 338 products arrived from the UAE.
  • Future: Talks with Indian manufacturers for local generic production.

Sir Ketumile Masire Hospital Becomes Public Referral Hub

Published: April 20, 2026 | Reporter: Maatla Francis

GABORONE — In a major restructuring of Botswana's healthcare system, the government has officially transitioned the state-of-the-art Sir Ketumile Masire Teaching Hospital (SKMTH) into a fully public referral and quaternary hub.The move is part of an urgent healthcare reset designed to dramatically expand public access to specialized medicine, clear staggering surgical backlogs, and relieve severe overcrowding at neighboring Princess Marina Hospital. A Massive Shift in Public HealthcareLocated on the grounds of the University of Botswana, the 450-bed facility was originally positioned as a government-owned private institution. Its rapid conversion to a fully public hub means that high-level, advanced treatments—previously restricted or financially out of reach for the average citizen—are now integrated into the public safety net. As Botswana's first designated quaternary hospital (a facility providing highly specialized, advanced forms of medicine and surgery), SKMTH will now serve as the absolute apex of the national referral system. Defusing the Pressure on Princess MarinaThe transition marks the first phase of a strategic plan to migrate critical, high-pressure services away from struggling regional facilities. Key departments have already begun standardizing public operations at SKMTH, including:Accident and emergency care Advanced radiology and dialysis More than 20 specialist outpatient clinics, covering cardiology, nephrology, neurology, and complex pediatric subspecialties. A primary focus of the public takeover is a targeted assault on long-standing elective surgery waitlists. By reallocating resources to SKMTH, the government has boosted orthopedic bed capacity from 66 to 106, effectively doubling the daily surgical output. Similar aggressive measures are being rolled out to address gynological backlogs, where hundreds of patients have historically faced prolonged delays for care.

Botswana's "Health Reset" - Can the New Strategy Save Princess Marina?

Published: MARCH 9, 2026

By: Maatla Francis

GABORONE — As of May 2026, Botswana finds itself at a critical crossroads. The nation is currently navigating a high-stakes "Public Health Reset" led by the administration of President Duma Boko. With the legendary Princess Marina Hospital (PMH) struggling under the weight of decades-old congestion and a fragile economy recovering from a 2024 contraction, the government is betting big on structural reform to restore public trust. The Great Decongestion: Turning SKMTH Public For years, Princess Marina Hospital has been the face of Botswana's healthcare crisis—defined by overflowing wards, surgical backlogs, and pharmacy queues that stretch into the parking lots. In a bold move that has sparked intense nationwide debate, the government officially completed the transition of the Sir Ketumile Masire Teaching Hospital (SKMTH) into a fully public facility this April.
